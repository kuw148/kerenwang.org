<h2><strong>ABOUT ME:</strong></h2>
<p>As a communication scholar, my research and teaching draw rhetorical theory, transnational legal studies, and political communication into a common analytical frame to address questions of contemporary governance and ethics. My monograph, <a href=”https://id.lib.harvard.edu/alma/99153820005603941/catalog”><em>Legal and Rhetorical Foundations of Economic Globalization: An Atlas of Ritual Sacrifice in Late-Capitalism</em></a> (Routledge, 2020), examines how ritualistic frameworks render the exploitative structures of the prevailing political-economic system inescapable—or even preferable—offering an interdisciplinary critical lens on the architecture of global development. My current research project maps how increasingly agentic “thinking machines” reshape sacrificial rationalities in governance, labor, the rule of law, and social development.</p>
