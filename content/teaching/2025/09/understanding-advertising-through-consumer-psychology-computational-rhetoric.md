<h2><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1065" height="719" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-header.gif" width="1280"/></h2>
<h2 class="c17" id="h.26n593d62ttq"><span class="c8 c29 c16" style="color: #ccffff;">What Do You See?</span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c8 c4">Let’s begin today’s lesson with a quick glance at these sets of images. What do they remind you of?</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1050" height="530" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertisements-Associations.gif" width="477"/></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c8 c4">Each image is a minimal arrangement of color blocks and geometric curves. No text, no logos, no slogans. And yet I suspect for many of us especially in the U.S., they evoke something instantly recognizable.</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">If a brand or product comes to mind, you're already encountering one of the most powerful mechanisms in modern advertising: </span><span class="c1">associative branding</span><span class="c4">. This is advertising stripped to its bare</span><span class="c1"> ritual function</span><span class="c4">.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt1" id="ftnt_ref1">[1]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> Rather than persuading us through spoken or written words and "rational" appeal, they operate by cultivating emotional resonance, by embedding themselves in our shared memory structures.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is the rhetorical power of</span><b> ritualizing brand familiarity and distinction with repetition</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and it sets the stage for our exploration of advertising as both a cultural artifact and an increasingly computational rhetorical craft.</span></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17" id="h.wyeqkw7puh5d"><span class="c8 c29 c16"><span style="color: #ccffff;">Historical "Thickness" of Advertising</span> </span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c8 c4">The history of advertising is deeply entangled with the evolution of media technology. From those street cries and painted signage of antiquity to the immersive digital campaigns of today, advertisements have always adapted to the dominant modes of public communication.</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">The </span><span class="c1">Industrial Revolution</span><span class="c4"> marked a pivotal turning point, giving rise to professional </span><span class="c1">advertising agencies</span><span class="c4"> that systematized commercial persuasion on a mass scale.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt2" id="ftnt_ref2">[2]</a></sup><span class="c4"> But the rapid proliferation of </span><span class="c1">false and misleading ads</span><span class="c4"> during the late 19th and early 20th century led to public outcry and the eventual creation of government oversight bodies tasked with regulating commercial speech across emerging communication infrastructures, including what would become the </span><strong><span class="c1">Federal Communications Commission</span></strong><span class="c4"> (FCC).</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt3" id="ftnt_ref3">[3]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">In the 21st century, we’ve seen the </span><span class="c1">decline of legacy ad agencies</span><span class="c4"> and the </span><span class="c1">rise of platform-based digital advertising</span><span class="c8 c4">, as evidenced by the following chart showing tech giants like Google and Meta turning user data into a significant share of the U.S. economy through targeted ad revenue.</span></p>
<p><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1051" height="880" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-chart-showing-tech-giants-like-Google-and-Meta-turning-user-data-into-a-significant-share-of-U.S.-economy-through-targeted-ad-revenue.png" width="962"/></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">This transformation coincides with the rise of </span><strong><span class="c1">associative ads</span></strong><span class="c4"> (which rely on emotional cues and brand mythologies) over older </span><span class="c1">demonstrative strategies</span><span class="c4"> that emphasized product features and unique benefits. Altogether, these shifts mark a sweeping turn toward </span><strong><span class="c1">hyper-personalization</span></strong><span class="c4"> and </span><strong><span class="c1">computational rhetoric</span></strong><span class="c4">, where machine learning systems continuously analyze behavioral data to generate ads optimized for each viewer’s psychological profile in real time.</span></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17 c22" id="h.4d5153j28kaw"><span class="c8 c29 c16" style="color: #ccffff;">Demonstrative and Associative Ads</span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c8 c4">Modern advertising increasingly drifts away from product demonstration and toward affective association. Let’s first pause briefly to clarify these two advertising modalities, by comparison the following two sets of advertisements:</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1052" height="1079" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-Demonstrative-vs-Associative-collage.png" width="1920"/></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">The left-side collage shows examples of </span><strong><span class="c1">demonstrative ads</span></strong><span class="c4"> focused on highlighting features and demonstrating its unique benefits. </span><span class="c1">Demonstrative advertising</span><span class="c8 c4"> operates through propositional logic: “Here’s the product, here’s what it does better than the rest.”</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Whereas in the collage on the right shows examples of </span><strong><span class="c1">associative ads</span></strong><span class="c4">, which rely on tapping into the audience's psychological structures to establish affective associations—what we might colloquially describe as "</span><span class="c4 c10">vibes</span><span class="c4">," "</span><span class="c4 c10">moods</span><span class="c4">," or "</span><span class="c4 c10">taste</span><span class="c4">."</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt4" id="ftnt_ref4">[4]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><strong><span class="c1">Associative advertising</span></strong><span class="c8 c4"> embed brand recognition in a dense network of cultural signifiers, emotions, and memory triggers. This is a defining characteristic of contemporary advertising aesthetics: it is increasingly common to see advertisements that rely on abstract, subtle psychological cues to reinforce positive emotional associations with the brand:</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<pre class="c3"><span class="c8 c4"><span style="color: #ffff99;">Consider Doritos’ 2021 “<strong>Brandless</strong>” campaign: they removed their logo entirely, relying instead on the consumer’s internalized memory structure to make the identification. </span><br/><img alt="" class="wp-image-1053 alignnone" height="426" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-Doritos-2021-Brandless-campaign-1024x670.png" width="651"/><br/></span></pre>
</div>
<div>
<pre class="c3"><span style="color: #99ccff;"><span class="c4">Even non-visual cues can activate these memory structures. </span><strong><span class="c1">Intel’s</span><span class="c4"> iconic </span><span class="c15 c4"><a class="c11" href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.myinstants.com/en/instant/intel-sound/&amp;sa=D&amp;source=editors&amp;ust=1759131353889304&amp;usg=AOvVaw3-pgbHuq7V3MY8y9jZ7f_I" style="color: #99ccff;">five-note chime</a></span><span class="c4"> [</span><span class="c15 c4"><a class="c11" href="https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.myinstants.com/en/instant/intel-sound/&amp;sa=D&amp;source=editors&amp;ust=1759131353889534&amp;usg=AOvVaw3cSgCvqpr9YhJEvTVX4O-k" style="color: #99ccff;">listen</a></span><span class="c4">]</span></strong><span class="c4">, for example, </span><span class="c4 c10">invokes</span><span class="c8 c4"> a brand through auditory ritual repetitions.</span></span></pre>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">In these instances of associative advertising, the brand becomes what Roland Barthes calls a "</span><strong><span class="c4 c10">mythic signifier</span></strong><span class="c4">," or a <em><strong>totemic object which what it symbolizes displaces what it actually sells</strong></em>.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt5" id="ftnt_ref5">[5]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17 c22" id="h.uh0fxgv2a95c"><span class="c8 c16 c29" style="color: #ccffff;">Ritual, Repetition, &amp; Consumer Psychology</span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">Thus, to study advertising is to study consumer psychology. In advertising theory, we often distinguish between </span><strong><span class="c1">differentiation</span></strong><span class="c4"> and </span><strong><span class="c1">distinction</span></strong><span class="c4">:</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt6" id="ftnt_ref6">[6]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></p>
</div>
<div>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul class="c21 lst-kix_me2gn4qi2jev-0 start">
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Differentiation</span></strong><span class="c4"><strong> </strong>answers the question: </span><em><span class="c27 c4 c10">Why should I choose this product over others?</span></em></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Distinction</span></strong><span class="c4"> answers the more basic question: </span><em><span class="c4 c10 c27">How do I even recognize this product at all?</span></em></span></pre>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Media research increasingly shows that distinction, not differentiation, tends to be the more powerful persuasive force in shaping consumer behavior. That’s why brands invest so heavily in repetition: not just to inform, but to </span><strong><span class="c1">ritualize</span></strong><span class="c4">, because being recognized in its symbolic form tends to matter more than being better in its material substance.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt7" id="ftnt_ref7">[7]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">This also explains why associative ads often outperform their demonstrative counterparts in building long-term brand recognition. They become </span><span class="c1">mnemonic devices</span><span class="c4"> that are triggered by <strong>repetitions of a color, a shape, a sound, a smell</strong> and so on. <span style="font-size: 10pt;">&lt;</span></span><span style="font-size: 10pt;"><span class="c5">Ayaz, Shaukat, Wisal Ahmad, and Mehboob ur Rashid. "Investigating the Comparative Effectiveness of Demonstrative and Straight-Sell Comparative Advertisements." </span><span class="c5 c10">The Journal of Humanities &amp; Social Sciences</span><span class="c5 c14"> 26, no. 1 (2018): 103-118.&gt;</span></span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Perhaps most importantly, advertising persuades by triangulatin</span><span class="c4">g the audience through structures of </span><span class="c1">social influence</span><span class="c8 c4">, drawing on matrices of collective norms and values.</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">It taps into powerful psychological tendencies of </span><strong><span class="c1">conformity</span></strong><span class="c4"><strong> </strong>(aligning our behavior with perceived group expectations), </span><strong><span class="c1">identification</span></strong><span class="c4"><strong> </strong>(shaping our tastes based on those we admire), and</span><span class="c1"> <strong>internalization</strong></span><span class="c4"><strong> </strong>(adopting preferences rooted in close or emotionally significant relations). This is why the most effective ad campaigns often don’t feel like forceful persuasion at all. Instead, they invoke or manufacture a sense of belonging.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt8" id="ftnt_ref8">[8]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1055" height="666" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-and-Consumer-Psychology.png" width="1543"/></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17 c22" id="h.qsu3bpa0yb2l"><span class="c8 c29 c16" style="color: #ccffff;">Bandwagon &amp; Anti-Bandwagon Effects</span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">One of the most familiar tools in the advertiser’s kit is the </span><strong><span class="c1">Bandwagon </span></strong><span class="c1">effect</span><span class="c4">, a widely observed tendency for an individual or groups of individuals to adopt behaviors or beliefs because “everyone else is doing it.”</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt9" id="ftnt_ref9">[9]</a></sup><span class="c4"> Social media further amplifies this phenomenon, making <strong>virality itself a potent </strong></span><strong><span class="c1">rhetorical resource</span></strong><span class="c4">.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt10" id="ftnt_ref10">[10]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">That said, a thing can turn into its opposite if pushed too far. Once a bandwagon becomes suffocating ubiquity, psychological </span><span class="c1">reactance </span><span class="c4">may kick in, and consumers would forcefully resist what feels like coercive overexposure. This phenomenon, known as the </span><span class="c1">anti-bandwagon effect</span><span class="c4">, can be understood as a psychological defense: a way of reclaiming personal autonomy and</span><span class="c1"> rhetorical agency</span><span class="c4"> by rejecting the overdetermination of </span><span class="c1">peer pressure</span><span class="c4">.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt11" id="ftnt_ref11">[11]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<pre class="c3"><span style="color: #cc99ff;"><span style="color: #99ccff;"><span class="c4">Advertisers have harnessed the resistance potential of <strong>anti-bandwagon</strong> effect as well. <strong>Apple</strong>’s famous </span><em><strong><span class="c4 c10">“1984</span></strong></em><span class="c4"><strong><em>”</em> campaign</strong> did exactly that: it sold its Macintosh computers not as the dominant product, but as a manufactured </span><span class="c4 c10">revolt against </span><span class="c4">the implied market domination of IBM at the time.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt12" id="ftnt_ref12" style="color: #99ccff;">[12]</a></sup></span><span class="c8 c4"><span style="color: #99ccff;"> </span><br/><br/><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-1056" height="245" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Apple-1984-Ad.gif" width="318"/></span></span></pre>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Thus, whether through forced conformity or rebellion, modern advertising finds its way to exploit our desire to differentiate ourselves.</span></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17 c22" id="h.vi7g0j3q3k9t"><span class="c8 c29 c16"><span style="color: #ccffff;">Social Marketing &amp; Exploitation</span> </span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Many companies now frame their messaging within narratives of social responsibility. This is called </span><strong><span class="c1">social marketing</span></strong><span class="c4">, where ads seek to advance not just sales by harnessing the narratives of civic virtues and the "common good."</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt13" id="ftnt_ref13">[13]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<pre class="c3"><span style="color: #ff99cc;"><span class="c4">As  we’ve seen in the case of </span><strong><span class="c1">Hershey’s </span><span class="c4">2021</span><span class="c1"> “Accountability, Transparency, Due Diligence” campaign</span></strong><span class="c4">, which served as a rhetorical camouflage precisely at a moment when Hershey's was under investigation for child labor violations and facing a class action lawsuit filed by former child slaves who alleged that the Pennsylvania-based company "aided and abetted their enslavement on cocoa plantations in Ivory Coast."</span><sup class="c28"><a href="#ftnt14" id="ftnt_ref14" style="color: #ff99cc;">[14]</a><br/><br/><img alt="" class="wp-image-1057 aligncenter" height="482" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Hershey-Social-Marketing-admist-child-labor-lawsuits.png" width="678"/></sup></span></pre>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Another common form of advertising spin, known as "</span><strong><span class="c1">greenwashing</span></strong><span class="c4">," specifically involves the use of “green” language to sanitize a corporation’s image and distract from its environmental infractions. </span></p>
<pre class="c3"><span style="color: #ccffcc;"><span class="c4">Many critics point to the </span><span class="c1">“Climate Change Collection”</span><span class="c4"> shown below as an example of <strong>IKEA</strong>’s social marketing campaigns to "<strong>greenwash</strong>" the company’s well-documented history of selling furniture made from illegally logged wood.</span><sup class="c28"><a href="#ftnt15" id="ftnt_ref15" style="color: #ccffcc;">[15]</a><br/><br/><img alt="" class="aligncenter wp-image-1058" height="377" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/ikea-the-climate-change-collection.jpg" width="669"/></sup></span></pre>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c8 c4">These "spin" practices demonstrate that advertising is far more than just another marketing tool. It is deeply enmeshed in questions of citizenship practice, legitimacy, power, and exploitation.</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c8 c4"><em>Speaking of exploitation</em>, perhaps few audiences are more vulnerable to ethically fraught advertising strategies than children.</span></p>
<figure>
  <img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-1060" height="883" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Balenciaga-2022-QAnon-coded-ad-campaign-controversy.png" width="612"/>
  <figcaption>Balenciaga 2022 QAnon coded ad campaign controversy - Safronova, Valeriya. “Balenciaga’s Ad Scandal, Explained.” The New York Times, November 28, 2022. https://www.nytimes.com/2022/11/28/style/balenciaga-campaign-controversy.html</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">A comprehensive report by the </span><span class="c1">American Psychological Association</span><span class="c4"> </span><span class="c4">shows that over half of ads on children’s programming promote </span><span class="c1">unhealthy foods</span><span class="c4"> (e.g., candy, cereal, fast food), often with no critical media literacy safeguards. Worse, these ads increasingly appear in the form of </span><span class="c1">advergames</span><span class="c4">, YouTube influencer videos, and viral content that blurs the line between entertainment and solicitation. Even schools are not immune: advertisers now enter classrooms via sponsored materials and exclusive vending contracts.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt16" id="ftnt_ref16">[16]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-1061" height="592" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-The-impact-of-food-advertising-on-childhood-obesity-APA-study.png" style="font-weight: bold; background-color: transparent; font-family: Inconsolata, monospace; font-size: 17px; text-align: inherit;" width="1250"/></p>
https://www.apa.org/topics/obesity/food-advertising-children</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">Beyond harmful consumption, children are also exploited along the </span><span class="c1">production chain</span><span class="c4">. Since 2009, the </span><span class="c1">U.S. Department of Labor</span><span class="c4"> has flagged global cocoa production as high-risk for </span><span class="c1">child labor and slavery</span><span class="c4">, particularly in West Africa. Much of this exploitation is driven by the </span><span class="c1">expanding cocoa processing operations</span><span class="c4"> of multinational food corporations based in the U.S. and Western Europe.</span><sup class="c30 c6"><a href="#ftnt17" id="ftnt_ref17">[17]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<figure>
  <img alt="" class="wp-image-1062 size-full" height="1456" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/US-Labor-Dept-report-on-Child-Labor-2025-02-17.png" width="1344"/>
  <figcaption>U.S. Department of Labor. Cocoa: Supply Chains. Bureau of International Labor Affairs. Accessed September 28, 2025. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ilab/reports/child-labor/list-of-goods/supply-chains/cocoa</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">Consequently, whether it’s in front of a screen or at the base of a supply chain, vulnerable populations such as children are routinely positioned not only as target audience of advertising, but also as its </span><span style="color: #ccffff;"><strong><span class="c1">sacrificial victims within the broader transnational economy of over-consumption</span></strong></span><span class="c4">.</span></p>
</div>
<div><hr/></div>
<div>
<h2 class="c17 c22" id="h.iidw96apa7dh"><span class="c8 c29 c16" style="color: #ccffff;">Computational Rhetoric of Hyper-Personalization</span></h2>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c19"><span class="c4">Finally, the rise of digital advertising in the 21st century has redefined what advertising </span><span class="c4 c10">is</span><span class="c8 c4"> by transforming how ads are generated, circulated, consumed, and modulated. This transformation has been propelled by several key technological breakthroughs:</span></p>
</div>
<div>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul class="c21 lst-kix_a0xj28b4cnts-0 start">
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Machine learning algorithms</span></strong><span class="c8 c4"><strong> </strong>capable of pattern recognition across massive data sets</span></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Predictive analytics</span></strong><span class="c8 c4"> that anticipate consumer behavior</span></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><span class="c1"><strong>Real-time bidding</strong> systems</span><span class="c8 c4"> for ad placement</span></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><span class="c4">And most recently, the rise of </span><strong><span class="c1">generative AI models</span></strong><span class="c8 c4"> capable of producing vast quantities of media content on demand.</span></span></pre>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Before we unpack this algorithmic pipeline, a methodological note: the technical machinery behind modern ad delivery often feels like a </span><strong><span class="c1">black box</span></strong><span class="c4"> system. Proprietary algorithms, inaccessible data streams, and closed feedback loops make it difficult for everyday users to grasp how these messages are generated and optimized. In this course, where </span><span class="c1">digital literacy</span><span class="c4"> is a central pedagogical focus, we will render this apparatus intelligible by articulating its </span><strong><span class="c1">computational rhetoric</span></strong><span class="c4">: a paradigm in which rhetorical invention is no longer at the helm of human agents alone, but programmed, synthesized, tested, and optimized through automated systems.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt18" id="ftnt_ref18">[18]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">Together, these innovations have enabled a shift toward </span><strong><span class="c1">hyper-personalization</span></strong><span class="c4">: the ability to tailor advertising not just to broad demographic segments, but to the evolving senses and sensibilities of individual users across platforms and temporal states.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt19" id="ftnt_ref19">[19]</a></sup><span class="c4"> Whether you're watching a streaming video, scrolling through short-form content, or browsing an e-commerce site, you are continuously presented with ads that adapt to your behavior, preferences, and context in real time.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt20" id="ftnt_ref20">[20]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<figure>
  <img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-1063" height="1403" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-Example-of-targeting-parameters-for-Google-Ads.png" width="1662"/>
  <figcaption>Example of targeting parameters for Google Ads</figcaption>
</figure>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">This brings us to a case study of an emerging advertising practice known as </span><strong><span class="c1">Dynamic Creative Optimization</span></strong><span class="c4">, or DCO, a process where ad content is generated, tested, and refined through a feedback loop of machine learning and user interaction.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt21" id="ftnt_ref21">[21]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">We begin with the </span><strong><span class="c1">exigence</span></strong><span class="c4">, by asking what material needs would necessitate computational advertising as appropriate means to fulfill? When the target audience is hidden in a</span><span class="c4 c10"> fragmented, attention-scarce media </span><span class="c4">ecosystem, advertisers must persuade individualized users across diverse platforms, at speed, and at scale. The rhetorical </span><strong><em><span class="c4 c10">purpose</span></em></strong><span class="c4">, then, becomes how to optimize attention capture and behavioral influence through automated means, in order to produce the most desired audience response to the underlying advertising needs.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt22" id="ftnt_ref22">[22]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">This exigence sets the</span><span class="c1"> <strong><em>scene</em></strong></span><span class="c4">, a datafied lifeworld that frames what <strong><em>actions</em></strong> are possible for a computational rhetorical response, an automated cycle in which symbolic appeals are generated, tested, and refined by machines rather than by human rhetors alone. </span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt23" id="ftnt_ref23">[23]</a></sup></p>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c8 c4"><strong>Let’s walk through this automated DCO cycle step by step:</strong><img alt="" class="alignright wp-image-1064" height="889" src="/images/uploads/2025/09/Advertising-DCO-432x1024.png" width="375"/></span></p>
</div>
<div>
<ol class="c21 lst-kix_e8mx7q9rrflx-0 start" start="1">
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><span class="c1"><strong>Data trail and audience modeling - </strong></span><span class="c4">The cycle begins with a user’s data trail, which includes behavior, location, demographics, search history, and contextual signals such as device and time of day. <span style="font-weight: 400;">Rhetorical </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">agents</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in this stage are platforms and data brokers. Their </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">agency</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> consists of trackers and SDKs. And the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">purpose</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for this step is predictive insight.</span></span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt24" id="ftnt_ref24" style="color: #ffcc99;">[24]</a></sup></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Situational analysis by machine learning - </span></strong><span class="c4">This data feeds models that analyze the rhetorical situation: Who is the user, what constraints are salient, and which appeals (e.g., logical, emotional, stylistic, sensorial) are likely to succeed?</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt25" id="ftnt_ref25" style="color: #ffcc99;">[25]</a></sup><span class="c4"> The ML model then fulfills its </span><span class="c4 c10">purpose</span><span class="c4"> of forecasted receptivity at this stage through automated </span><span class="c4 c10">acts</span><span class="c4"> of segmentation and inference.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt26" id="ftnt_ref26" style="color: #ffcc99;">[26]</a></sup></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Generating content variants </span></strong><span class="c4">-</span><span class="c1"> </span><span class="c4">With user insights in hand, the system selects user-adapted visuals, tones, narratives, and styles, then generates multiple tailored ad variants for testing. Generative models can serve as automated</span><span class="c4 c10"> agents</span><span class="c4"> to fabricate, copy, and layout content at scale. The </span><span class="c4 c10">purpose </span><span class="c4">for this step is </span><span class="c1">identification</span><span class="c4"> – to achieve a measure of connection (<em>consubstantiality</em>) with the target user's values, beliefs, and interests.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt27" id="ftnt_ref27" style="color: #ffcc99;">[27]</a></sup></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Live testing and performance measurement</span></strong><span class="c4"><strong> </strong>- Content variants are deployed for real time A/B testing. Metrics such as click‑through rate, view duration, and even user conversion and eye-movements are collected and compared. <span style="font-weight: 400;">The rhetorical </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">act</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for this step is real time automated messaging trials within a live </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">scene</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of platforms and feeds. The networked testing infrastructure and auction systems provides the rhetorical <em>agency</em>. The <i>purpose</i> for this stage is automated evidentiary calibration, and the <b>scene–purpose ratio</b> shows how the platform environment channels which outcomes are legible and valuable</span>.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt28" id="ftnt_ref28" style="color: #ffcc99;">[28] </a></sup></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Feedback, learning, and refinement</span></strong><span class="c4"> - Testing data then flows back into the system. Both the message and the model are tuned through iterative rehearsal and user optimization. The rhetorical </span><span class="c4 c10">act</span><span class="c4"> in this step is recalibration through the means (agency) of its optimization algorithms, in order to fulfill the </span><span class="c4 c10">purpose</span><span class="c4"> of improved fit.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt29" id="ftnt_ref29" style="color: #ffcc99;">[29]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></span></pre>
</li>
<li class="c3 c13 li-bullet-0">
<pre><span style="color: #ffcc99;"><strong><span class="c1">Targeted delivery at the moment of engagement</span></strong><span class="c4"> - </span><span class="c4">The best‑performing content is then served in real time to tailor the user’s sensibilities at the present moment, maximizing user's emotional resonance and behavioral response to the advertisement. <span style="font-weight: 400;">The rhetorical </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">act</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> here is finely timed content delivery within a dynamic </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">scene</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Ad servers and recommendation engines act as </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">agents</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, delivering ads in real-time for the </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">purpose</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of steering users to purchase, sign‑up, or share</span>.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt30" id="ftnt_ref30" style="color: #ffcc99;">[30]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></span></pre>
</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div>
<p class="c3"><span class="c4">In this sense, computational systems do more than execute a content strategy. DCO represents a fully automated staged </span><strong><span class="c1">drama of motives</span></strong><span class="c4"> in which <strong>algorithmic rituals</strong>, tools, settings, and advertisers' aims co‑determine the persuasive act in real-time, with minimum human intervention.</span><sup class="c4"><a href="#ftnt31" id="ftnt_ref31">[31]</a></sup><span class="c8 c4"> </span></p>
</div>
