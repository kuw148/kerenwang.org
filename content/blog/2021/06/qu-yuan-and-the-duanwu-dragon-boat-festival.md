Happy <strong>端午節</strong> Duanwu / Dragon Boat Festival!

<img alt="Qu Yuan's Lisao (Image source Wikimedia Commons)" class="aligncenter wp-image-550 size-large" height="430" src="/images/uploads/2021/06/Lisao-1024x648.png" width="680"/>
<em>Duanwu</em> is one of the four most important traditional holidays (the other three being <em>Qingming,</em> Mid Autumn Festival and the Spring Festival/Chinese New Year) celebrated by Chinese people throughout the world. It is celebrated on the <strong>5th day of the 5th month of traditional Chinese calendar,</strong> which for 2021 falls on June 14th of the Gregorian calendar. The two calendars are not perfectly synched, so the Gregorian calendar date for Duanwu would change from year to year (FYI it will be on June 3rd for 2022, and June 22nd for 2023).

The <em>Duanwu</em> festival is primarily for commemorating the <a href="https://dragonboathistory.com/Legends.html">tragic death</a> of Warring States era poet and statesman <strong>Qu Yuan</strong> (c. 243-348 BC). Qu Yuan is one of the most celebrated literary figure in Chinese history, whose poems are known for their highly expressive lyrical style, imbibed with intoxicating <em>mythos</em> and broiling <em>pathos.</em> Qu Yuan is also among those <a href="https://www.scmp.com/article/703796/long-history-cutting-sleeves"> historical figures</a> became widely celebrated by the contemporary <a href="https://gayasianews.wordpress.com/2014/06/03/chinas-duanwu-festival-is-worlds-first-gay-valentines-day/">Chinese LGBTQ community</a>, as Qu frequently (also quite explicitly) writes about his <a href="https://ctext.org/chu-ci/si-mei-ren/zh">romantic passions towards his lover and patron King Huai of Chu</a>, whom Qu Yuan refers to as "my beautiful one."
